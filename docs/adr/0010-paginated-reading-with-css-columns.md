# Paginated reading with CSS columns on the article

The reader shows a document as pages that fit the window, one or two at a time, with no scrollbar, as print-like web readers do (WeChat Read, Kindle Cloud Reader). Long scrolls make it hard to keep one's place and to judge how much is left. We decided to lay the article out in CSS columns and move through it by its horizontal scroll position, keeping the text in the main document (ADR-0001). The scroll layout stays as a reading setting until the paginated layout has proven itself; it may then be removed.

- **Layout.** A reading setting, next to font size, line spacing and text width, with two values: *pages* (the default) and *scroll* (today's reader). It is stored per user on the server like the other reading settings, so it follows the reader across devices. Saved settings without a layout read as *pages*.
- **Page and spread.** In the pages layout the article is as tall as the window minus the reader's controls, and its text flows into columns. Each column is a page, as wide as the text width setting, or as wide as the space available when that is narrower (a phone): the text width setting stays a maximum, as in the scroll layout. A spread is the pages shown at once: two when the space available holds two pages at the text width setting plus the gap between them, otherwise one. The document page itself never scrolls; only the article does, horizontally, with its scrollbar hidden.
- **Spread start.** A spread opens with its spread start: the first line of its first page, which is a line of text or a block such as a picture or heading. The reader decides it; the column flow from the top of the document does not. To show a spread, the Navigator puts one empty marker, `<span data-tomeshelf-page-start>` with `display: block` and `break-before: column`, right before the spread start, so the browser begins a column there, and scrolls the article to that column. When the spread start is a block's first line, the marker goes before the block, so the block keeps its own indent and loses the margin above it, as at any page top. Otherwise it splits the text node at the line's first character, and the line keeps the look it had inside its paragraph, with no first-line indent. The article holds at most one marker; moving it merges the split text node again. The browser still breaks lines and lays out everything after the marker. Columns before the spread start are laid out but never shown, so where they break does not matter. A document's first spread starts at its first line and needs no marker.
  - *Where the marker goes.* When the spread start is the first line of several nested blocks (`<li><p>`, `<blockquote><p>`, a `<section>` opening with a heading), the marker goes before the outermost of them. The marker never goes inside a nested scroll area (a wide table or `pre`), a picture, `svg`, `video` or `hr`: each is one block that cannot split, so a spread starts at its start. Inside inline markup (`em`, `a`, `span`) the text node is split where it is, and the inline box continues after the marker. A poem's line (`p > span`, shown as a block with a hanging indent) counts as a block: when the spread start is its first line, the marker goes before the line's `span`, after the `br` that ends the line before it, so the line keeps its hanging indent; a wrapped continuation of the line splits its text node and stays indented. The marker is not styled as a poem line.
  - *Scroll end.* The article's scrollable width ends at the end of its content or at the end of the shown spread, whichever is further, so scrolling to the spread start's column never clamps and the last spread of a document is always a full spread. The reader sets it after placing the marker. It adds no element to the article.
- **Moving by scroll position, not by transform.** The visible spread is the article's `scrollLeft`. Because the article is an ordinary scroll container, `element.scrollIntoView()`, the browser's find-in-page, fragment links and a word-lookup extension jumping back to a sentence all bring the text into view without knowing about pages. When something scrolls the article to an offset that is not a spread's start, the reader, once the scroll ends, makes the first visible line the spread start: the line holding the first visible character, or the first visible block when no character is visible (a document of pictures). A table of contents entry or a link with a fragment, which the reader follows itself, makes the target element's first line the spread start, so the target opens the left page. The article takes `overflow-x: hidden`: it stays a scroll container that these and the reader move, but the browser never scrolls it on its own, so neither an unprevented wheel gesture nor a mouse selection dragged to its edge (the browser's selection autoscroll) can carry a spread away.
- **Turning spreads.** The Previous and Next controls, the Left/Right arrow keys, Page Up/Page Down, Space and Shift+Space, a horizontal swipe on touch screens, and one wheel or trackpad gesture each turn one spread. Clicking or tapping the text or the blank space beside it never turns a spread: clicks on the text belong to the reader and to extensions (ENX looks a word up on click), and a click on the text or beside a line marks that line (ADR-0005). Input that belongs to something else is left to it:
  - Keys do not turn a spread when focus is in a form control, button, link, radio group or editable element (the settings panel's theme radios use the arrow keys), or when Ctrl, Alt or Meta is held, or Shift with any key but Space, so browser shortcuts and selection keys keep working.
  - Ctrl + wheel, including trackpad pinch gestures reported as Ctrl + wheel, is left to the browser for zooming. It neither turns a spread nor changes the wheel gesture's quiet interval.
  - A wheel or swipe over a nested scroll area (a wide table or `pre`) that can still scroll in the gesture's direction scrolls that area; at its edge, or in a direction it cannot scroll, the gesture turns a spread.
  - One gesture turns at most one spread. A swipe is one touch from start to end, horizontal and long enough to be deliberate. Wheel events, including trackpad momentum, belong to one gesture until the wheel has been quiet for a short interval (about 300 ms); a burst turns one spread, however long it lasts, and so cannot cross into a second document.
  - The reading setting *Wheel turns pages* (on by default) can switch the wheel off, for readers whose resting hand or momentum turns spreads by accident. When it is off, wheel and trackpad gestures over the text turn no spread and are not default-prevented; the article's `overflow-x: hidden` keeps the browser from scrolling it natively. It stays a scroll container: `scrollIntoView()`, find-in-page, fragment links, keys, the controls and swipes still move it, and nested scroll areas still scroll under the wheel. The setting is stored per user on the server like the other reading settings; saved settings without it read as on.
- **Repeated lines.** A reading setting from 0 to 3, 1 by default, stored per user like the others (saved settings without it read as 1): how many lines a turn repeats. One line matches what readers that repeat lines do (KOReader's default page overlap is about one line; a browser's Page Down keeps one or two); a reader who selects sentences at spread ends can raise it. Next makes the line N lines before the end of the spread the new spread start (with 0, the first line after the spread), so the last N lines of one spread open the next; it always moves forward by at least one line. Previous shows the spread that ends with the current spread's N-th line (with 0, the line just before the spread). A turn never skips a line. When no spread ends exactly on that line, because a picture cannot split or a break rule moves lines together, Previous takes the earliest spread start whose spread reaches it, and so repeats more lines. When that would need text before the document's first line, Previous shows the document's first spread, from its first line, and repeats more.
- **Spread memory.** While the layout is unchanged, the reader remembers the spread starts it turned through, back and forward, as a browser's history does, so Next then Previous, or Previous then Next, returns exactly the spread the user saw. Several spread starts can end on the same line when a spread has blank space at its foot, so computing Previous again could show a fuller spread instead. Re-layout, a reading setting change, opening a document, a table of contents entry or link, and a scroll the reader did not make (find-in-page, `scrollIntoView()`) clear the memory. Only a Previous with no remembered spread computes it: the Navigator tries spread starts before the current one, measures where each spread ends, and searches for the earliest that reaches the line.
- **Ends of a document.** Next on the last spread opens the next document at its first spread; Previous on the first spread opens the previous document at its last spread. The address stays one per document (ADR-0005); arriving at the last spread is navigation state, not part of the address. That last spread is the one that ends with the document's last line, found as Previous finds a spread.
- **Re-layout.** A change of window size, a reading setting or a late-loading font changes where pages break. The spread start stays the first line of the first page: the rest of that page and any second page re-flow, and the spreads before and after are found from it. Page numbers and the page count change with the layout. The reader keeps the spread start as state from the last turn or alignment; it does not measure the first visible character after the window has already changed. When the user has clicked or selected on the spread (the clicked character, ADR-0005) and its line is no longer on the re-laid spread, the spread start moves to the line *Repeated lines* lines before the clicked line (with 0, the clicked line itself; with fewer lines before it in the document, the document's first line), so the clicked line shows near the top with the lines that lead into it. When that spread does not reach the clicked line, because a line before it is a block that cannot split (a picture as tall as a page), the spread start steps forward one line at a time, at most to the clicked line itself, until the clicked line is on the spread. While the clicked line stays on the spread, the spread start stays, so the text moves as little as possible. Restoring a saved position follows the same rule.
- **Reading mark.** One arrow in the margin marks the line of the reading position: the clicked character's line after a click or selection, otherwise the spread start. There is never more than one. It moves to the clicked line on a click, to the new spread start on a turn or any other new spread start, and stays on its line through re-layout. It sits at the height of the clicked character itself, even inside a block that cannot split (a table or `pre`); without one, beside the top of the spread start, a line or a block such as a picture. It points at the text. It is drawn in an overlay outside the article, so the article's text and markup do not change and extensions never see it (ADR-0006); it takes no pointer events and is hidden from assistive technology. On a two-page spread it sits in the outer margin of the page that holds its line: left of the left page, pointing right, when the line is on the left page; right of the right page, pointing left, when the line is on the right page. Without a clicked character it is always beside the left page, where the spread start is. With one page it sits left of the page. The scroll layout has no reading mark.
- **Page numbers.** Below the spread, between Previous and Next, the reader shows the spread's page numbers and the book's page count, "12–13 of 340" on a two-page spread and "12 of 340" on one page; a two-page spread whose second page holds none of the document shows one number. They are estimates, cheap enough to update on every turn: the reader never lays out, fetches or renders another document to count its pages. Hovering over them says "Page numbers are estimates and change with window size and reading settings."
  - *Document text counts.* For each document of the reading order, the Streamer counts its characters and its pictures each time it serves the manifest. Characters are the non-whitespace UTF-16 code units of the `body`'s text, leaving out `script` and `style`, as ADR-0005 counts offsets. Pictures are the `img`, `svg` and `video` elements not inside an `svg`, the blocks that cannot split, so an `svg` wrapping an `image` is one picture. The manifest's reading-order links carry the counts, so the contract test of ADR-0012 holds both Streamers to the same numbers. Counting takes a few milliseconds for a novel, once per book opened, so a private instance keeps no counts on disk and has no derived file to remove with the book (ADR-0007). On a public instance the browser Streamer counts them in the browser (ADR-0012), so no count or page number reaches the server.
  - *Measured documents.* The current document's pages are counted in the current layout: the spread start's page is its column, counting from 1, and the document's page count is the number of columns up to its last line. Placing the marker changes the column flow, so the reader keeps each shown document's latest count, not the one it had when it opened.
  - *Characters per page.* A document's text pages are its pages less half a page per picture. The ratio is the characters of all documents of this book measured in the current layout divided by their text pages, once those text pages reach 4 and the characters are more than 0; a cover, a book of pictures or a few short documents would give a meaningless ratio. A document whose text pages are 0 or fewer (a page or two of ornaments or icons) is left out of the ratio, its characters and text pages both, and still counts its measured pages.
  - *Other documents.* A document not measured in the current layout is estimated as its characters divided by the characters per page, plus half a page per picture, rounded and at least 1 page. With no ratio yet, the reader shows page numbers only when every document of the book is measured, and otherwise none. Reading forward never makes the spread number jump: the first page of a document follows the last page of the one before. Only the page count changes, when a document's measured count replaces its estimate.
  - *Scope.* Measured counts and the ratio belong to one book in one tab and one layout. Anything that re-lays out the document (window size, a reading setting that changes the layout, a late-loading font) forgets them; a setting that does not, such as the theme or *Wheel turns pages*, keeps them. A picture that loads late reflows only its own document, which is measured again; the other documents keep their counts.
  - *When they change.* After a turn, a new spread start and re-layout. Finding the document's last line reads the existing layout from its last character back (a nested scroll area counts where its own box is), so showing them adds no layout pass. A spread number is never more than the page count. They are not part of the Locator and are not stored. The scroll layout shows no page numbers.
- **Breaks.** Images are scaled to be no taller than a page and are never split across pages. A figure stays on one page when it fits; a figure taller than a page (a long caption) keeps its image whole on one page and lets its caption continue on the next, so nothing is clipped or needs a vertical scroll. A heading is not left alone at the foot of a page.

How the reading position is measured in the pages layout is in ADR-0005.

## Considered Options

- **Transform the article (`translateX`) inside a clipping box**, as foliate-js and Readium do inside their iframes. Rejected: a transform is invisible to `scrollIntoView`, find-in-page and extensions, so ENX's jump back to a sentence would land on a hidden page.
- **Compute page breaks in JavaScript** by measuring blocks and moving them into page elements. Rejected: it rewrites the DOM the contract numbers (ADR-0006) and is slow on long documents; CSS columns break inside paragraphs for free.
- **An iframe per spread**, the usual paginated reader. Rejected by ADR-0001.
- **Pages counted from the top of the document**, the column flow alone, as this ADR first decided. Rejected after use: a resize moved the user's first line into the middle of another page, and a turn could not repeat lines, so a sentence cut at a spread's end could not be read or selected on one spread (issue #131).
- **Copy the repeated lines into the page**, inside the article or as generated content in a strip above it. Rejected: ENX and find-in-page would see the text twice, and generated content cannot be selected or looked up.
- **One column seen through one window per page.** Rejected: a node renders in one place, so two pages side by side would need a second copy of the whole document.
- **A spacer before the article's text** (`::before` with a computed height) instead of a marker. It pins a line without touching the text, but one fixed layout cannot show a line on two spreads, so it cannot repeat lines.
- **Our own line breaking and layout.** Rejected: it reimplements the browser's text layout, and positioned line elements would rewrite the DOM the contract numbers (ADR-0006). Only pagination, choosing where each spread starts, is ours.
- **On re-layout, keep the clicked line with the least movement**, so the re-laid spread ends on it. Rejected: the clicked line would sit at the foot of a page and the user would turn again at once.
- **On re-layout, always make the clicked line the spread start.** Rejected: the text jumps even when the clicked line is still on the spread, and the lines that lead into it move to the previous spread.
- **Count the pages of every document by laying it out off-screen** after each re-layout. Rejected: it fetches and lays out the whole book on every resize or setting change, which stalls the reader on a long book.
- **Estimate other documents from their uncompressed size in the EPUB**, the size total progression weights by (ADR-0005). Rejected: markup and picture references count as text, so a document heavy with them counted many more pages than it showed.
- **Readium's positions list** (one position per 1,024 bytes of each document), as Readium apps show "location N of M". Rejected: its count stays fixed when the layout changes, and the owner wants pages that follow the window and settings (issue #128); the list is also large to fetch for a long book.
- **Page numbers of the current document only.** Rejected by the owner: the reader wants to see how long the whole book is.
- **Keep scrolling only.** Rejected by the owner after trying v0.1.0: the scroll is the reason the reader feels unlike a book.

## Consequences

- The article becomes a horizontal scroll container in the pages layout. Text on other pages is in the DOM but outside the article's visible box. The DOM contract (`docs/dom-contract.md`) tells extension authors to bring text into view with `scrollIntoView()` and to measure visibility against the article's box, not the window; a Playwright test pins that `scrollIntoView()` on a block on a later page shows that page.
- The reading position work (issue #10) is redone for the pages layout, following ADR-0005 as revised with this ADR.
- Page numbers and the page count (issue #128) are estimates. Within a document they follow the spread start and change on re-layout. A document not yet shown in the current layout is estimated from its text counts, so opening the book in the middle, or a table of contents entry far ahead, can show a spread number that changes once the documents before it are shown; reading forward does not. The Streamer counts each document's text each time it serves the manifest.
- The article may hold an empty marker and a split text node. Neither changes its text or block numbers; ADR-0006 tells extensions not to rely on text node identity.
- A throwaway prototype (branch `prototype/anchor-pagination`, `web/prototypes/`) checked this on Chromium over 2,600 random turns and resizes: the spread start never moved, no line was skipped, the text never changed, and with the memory every round trip returned the same spread. Without the memory 73% did. A computed Previous repeated exactly N lines 86% of the time; the rest repeated more, mostly around pictures.
- If the scroll layout is removed later, the scroll measures in ADR-0005 go with it.

## Revisions

- Issue #57 spec review: the layout setting is stored per user on the server, as the other reading settings already are (it had wrongly said per browser); the page width is capped by the space available; keys, nested scroll areas and gesture momentum are defined for turning spreads; a figure taller than a page keeps its image whole and lets its caption continue.
- Issue #96 spec review: the wheel can be switched off by the *Wheel turns pages* setting; when off, the article takes `overflow-x: hidden` so an unprevented horizontal gesture cannot scroll it natively and be snapped into a turn.
- Issue #131: the article takes `overflow-x: hidden` whatever the wheel setting. With the wheel on, a mouse selection dragged to the article's edge made the browser autoscroll it, and the scroll was snapped into a turn that lost the selection. Nothing relied on native horizontal scrolling: the wheel and swipes already turn spreads in script.
- 2026-11-23, issue #138: a spread opens at a spread start the reader chooses and keeps through re-layout, with *Repeated lines* (default 1) on a turn and a memory of turned spreads, since a resize moved the first line the user was reading and a sentence cut at a spread's end could not be selected (#131). The spread start is shown with one empty marker element, so line breaking stays with the browser.
- 2026-11-23, issue #138 spec review: the marker goes before the outermost block a line starts and never inside a block that cannot split (a picture, a nested scroll area), and the article's scroll end reaches the end of the shown spread, since a marker inside an `li` would leave its bullet or number on the previous column, a marker inside a table cell or a picture cannot start a column, and a scroll clamped one column short would put the spread start on the wrong page; a poem line is a block for the marker, since a marker inside it would lose its hanging indent; a scroll with no visible character aligns to the first visible block.
- 2026-11-24, issue #129: re-layout and restoring keep the line the user last clicked or selected on the spread, with *Repeated lines* lines above it when it has to move, and a reading mark shows the line of the reading position, since a two-page to one-page resize kept the left page and lost the right page the user was reading (#130).
- 2026-11-24, PR #144 review: when a block that cannot split before the clicked line would push it off the re-laid spread, the spread start steps forward until the clicked line shows, and fewer lines before it than *Repeated lines* start the spread at the document's first line; the reading mark sits at the clicked character's height, or beside the top of the spread start, since a tall picture among the repeated lines hid the clicked line on a one-page spread.
- 2026-11-24, issue #129 (owner): there is one reading mark, in the outer margin of the page holding its line, never one on each page, since "left of the left page or right of the right page" read as if both could show; a click in the blank space beside a line also marks it and never turns a spread (ADR-0005).
- 2026-11-24, issue #128: the reader shows estimated page numbers and a book page count, counted in the current layout for documents it has shown and from visible characters and pictures for the rest, since laying out every document would stall the reader on each re-layout and uncompressed size counted markup as text.
- 2026-11-24, PR #147 review: the characters per page come from all measured documents once their text pages reach 4, a shown document keeps its latest count, counts and ratio belong to one book and layout, and characters and pictures are defined for both Streamers, since a picture-heavy document divided by zero, a book of short documents never showed numbers, and the marker changes a document's count while it is read. A follow-up review left documents with no text pages out of the ratio, since many small pictures gave them negative text pages that shrank the pool and hid the numbers.
- 2026-11-25, issue #128: the Streamer counts the text each time it serves the manifest instead of keeping the counts on disk, since counting a novel takes about 5–7 ms and a kept file would need its own lock and removal with the book.
- 2026-11-25, PR #148 review: a late-loading picture keeps the other documents' counts, since it reflows only its own document and forgetting them made the spread number jump; a nested scroll area at a document's end counts where its box is, since its text overflows past its column.
